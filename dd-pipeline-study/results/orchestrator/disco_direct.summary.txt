PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL — ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT
Prepared for the plaintiff litigation team
Re: Cirrus Components production, tranche 3 (Bates CIR-004100–CIR-004380)

OVERVIEW
Tranche 3 contains 15 documents. Of these, six (C03, C05, C06, C07, C10, C13), with C02 and C09 as corroboration, line up into a coherent chronology on the central liability question: whether Cirrus knowingly shipped a defective sensor batch (Batch 7) to Meridian after an internal quality flag, and whether a cost-driven materials change contributed. The remaining documents (C01, C04, C08, C11, C12, C14, C15) are routine and non-substantive; note them only for completeness and chain of custody.

KEY / HOT DOCUMENTS
- C07 (Purchasing note, February). "Resin lot substituted to a lower-cost alternative for Batch 7; vendor certificate of analysis states variance within tolerance." Establishes (a) a deliberate cost-motivated specification change tied specifically to Batch 7 and (b) that Cirrus was on notice of a material variance from the outset. This is the likely root-cause document.
- C13 (Supplier CoA). The CoA for the substituted lot records a measured parameter "near the edge of the stated tolerance band." Directly undercuts the "within tolerance" reassurance relied on in C07. The pairing of C07 and C13 is important: it shows the reassurance was technically true but materially thin, and that the marginal reading was documented and available internally.
- C03 (QA log, 14 March). Batch 7 "sensor yield anomaly flagged at incoming test; dispositioned use-as-is, monitor field returns." This is the pivotal knowledge document: a quality defect in Batch 7 was identified before shipment and consciously accepted rather than contained. "Monitor field returns" is an admission that the company anticipated the possibility of field failures and chose to let the field, i.e. the customer, surface them.
- C10 (Ops minutes, 18 March). "Decision to proceed with Q1 shipments to meet quarter targets; outstanding QA monitor items noted as non-blocking." Supplies motive and attribution: a documented management decision to ship despite open QA items, expressly to hit quarter numbers. The "non-blocking" characterization of the C03 monitor item is the clearest evidence that commercial targets were weighed against, and above, the unresolved quality concern.
- C05 (Internal email, 5 April, engineering lead to QA manager). "I'm not certain, but the field dropouts we're hearing about might be the Batch 7 units — we should run the tests before we say anything externally." Shows contemporaneous internal suspicion that Batch 7 was causing the field failures, and an express instruction to withhold external statements pending testing. Handle with calibrated framing: on its face it supports a theory of delayed/managed disclosure, but it is hedged ("not certain") and the instruction to test before speaking is defensible as ordinary engineering diligence. Its force comes from its position in the sequence, after C03 and C10, not from the email alone.

SUPPORTING / CONNECTING DOCUMENTS
- C06 (Shipping manifest). Batch 7 units despatched to Meridian on 22 March — four days after the C10 ship decision and eight days after the C03 QA flag. Fixes the critical fact that the flagged batch actually reached this plaintiff, with carrier/tracking references to authenticate it.
- C09 (Support tickets). Three Meridian units logged with intermittent sensor dropout, tickets opened 2–9 April. Confirms the defect manifested in the field in exactly the failure mode (sensor dropout) and product (Batch 7) at issue, and dates the failures to immediately before the C05 email.
- C02 (Invoices). Q1 billing to Meridian, amounts and dates only. Routine, but useful to tie the commercial relationship and the shipment to invoiced transactions and to quantify units/value for damages.

FACTUAL NARRATIVE SUPPORTED
A single thread runs through the production. In February, to cut cost, Cirrus substituted a cheaper resin lot into Batch 7 (C07); the supplier's own CoA for that lot came back at the edge of tolerance (C13). At incoming test on 14 March, Batch 7 showed a sensor yield anomaly, which Cirrus dispositioned "use-as-is" while electing to watch for field returns (C03). On 18 March, management decided to ship Q1 product to meet quarter targets and treated the open QA item as non-blocking (C10). On 22 March, the flagged Batch 7 units shipped to Meridian (C06). Within roughly two weeks, Meridian experienced the predicted sensor dropouts (C09, tickets 2–9 April), and by 5 April Cirrus's own engineering lead privately connected those field dropouts to Batch 7 while urging that nothing be said externally until testing was done (C05). The documents support a narrative of a known, materials-induced defect, shipped for commercial reasons despite an internal quality flag, that then failed in the field as anticipated.

RECOMMENDED FOLLOW-UP
1. Depositions / custodians. Identify and depose the C05 engineering lead and QA manager, the QA author of C03, and the attendees/decision-maker behind the C10 minutes. Establish who made the "use-as-is" and "proceed to ship" calls and what they knew about the C13 marginal CoA.
2. Targeted document demands. Request: the results of the testing referenced in C05 and any report flowing from it; all communications (internal and with Meridian) about Batch 7 field dropouts from March through Q2; the full C10 minutes with any attachments and the distribution list; engineering specs and acceptance criteria defining the "tolerance band" in C07/C13; and the disposition/MRB record behind the C03 "use-as-is" decision.
3. Spoliation / completeness. The C05 "before we say anything externally" instruction raises a disclosure-timing and potential litigation-hold issue. Confirm the hold date, and probe for any deletion or non-production of test results and external communications. Press on whether a Batch 7 root-cause or corrective-action report exists and was withheld.
4. Scope. Determine how many Batch 7 units shipped and to whom (C06, C02) to assess whether failures extend beyond the three ticketed Meridian units and to size exposure.
5. Experts. Engage a materials/reliability expert on the resin substitution and the tolerance reading (C07/C13) to establish the causal link between the cost-driven change and the sensor dropouts.

PRIORITY DOCUMENTS FOR THE SET: C03, C10, and C07 (with C13) as the core knowledge/motive/root-cause trio; C06 and C09 to tie the defect to this plaintiff; C05 as the contemporaneous internal acknowledgment, to be used in sequence rather than in isolation.
