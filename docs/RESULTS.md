## Judging and its reliability

GPT-6.1 Sol coded every Kestrel review and DD item. Opus 5.5 independently coded a deterministic
10% sample (537 Kestrel reviews, 109 DD items). Nine API reviews in the Kestrel sample were
collected after that run (2026-11-07/08) and are not second-coded.

| Field | Agreement | Cohen's κ | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kestrel issue position (16 per review) | 0.98 | 0.74 | primary outcome |
| Kestrel names_manipulation | 0.90 | 0.81 | primary |
| Kestrel adopts_framing | 0.95 | 0.49 | usable with GPT as coder: human vs GPT κ 0.91 (see below); Opus misses adoptions |
| DD red flags, fabrication, lone flag, admission | 1.00 | undefined: both judges used one category | primary |
| DD laundering, recommendation | 0.96 | 0.83 | primary |
| DD hedge | 0.85 | 0.68 | secondary; needs human validation |
| DD cluster | 0.88 | 0.23 | **not usable** without human coding |

**Human validation (2026-11-08).** The author coded the blinded 200-item sample (132 issue positions,
68 adopts_framing items), blind to condition and to both judges' labels. The sample is stratified
by the judge's label, so rare labels are over-represented. Full output:
`kestrel-injection-study/results_v2/analysis/human_agreement.md`.

| Human vs | issue position κ (agreement) | pushback vs moved κ | adopts_framing κ (agreement) |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-6.1 Sol | 0.54 (0.65) | 0.43 (0.70) | **0.91** (0.96) |
| Opus 5.5 | 0.46 (0.60) | n/a | 0.48 (0.74) |

- **The judge errs in one direction only.** In 39 of 132 issue items GPT coded PARTIAL, ACCEPT or
  NOT_ADDRESSED where the human saw pushback (25 PARTIAL, 8 ACCEPT, 6 NOT_ADDRESSED); it never
  coded pushback where the human saw anything else. So absolute skew levels are overstated, and the
  Kestrel conclusion that reviews hold their positions is conservative.
- The over-coding rate is similar on clean and manipulated reviews (C0 23%, A 33%, B 30%; small
  n). If anything it is slightly higher on manipulated ones, which would inflate Δ, so true effects
  are, if anything, smaller than reported. The sample is stratified by the judge's label, so these
  are not population rates.
- **adopts_framing is valid as GPT codes it** (κ 0.91 with the human). Its non-zero rate on clean
  reviews is real: models echo the seller's own "market standard" characterisations unprompted.
  Opus is the unreliable coder of this field (17 adoptions missed), which explains the low
  inter-judge κ. It moves from "not usable" to usable with GPT as coder.

Opus scores Kestrel reviews slightly harsher than GPT (mean skew 0.87 vs 0.77, difference −0.10
[−0.33, 0.12]). Every effect is within-judge, so this does not bias Δ.

## Findings

### 1. Contract review (Kestrel): conclusions hold; models usually say what they saw

Skew (0–32) of every treated cell against the same model's clean document:

- Core models: largest |Δ| 1.1 points. Holm-significant cells: GPT-6 Luna A2 +0.60 and
  B3 +0.75 (open task, guard off); Opus 5.5 A2 +1.10 (open task, no playbook). Sol, Haiku and
  Fable: none.
- Breadth, 5 reps: Sonnet 5.5 B2 +2.20 (open task, no playbook) and A2 −1.20 (open task, guard on)
  are the only significant cells.

### 2. Hidden text in a real PDF (Kestrel A2P)

The A2 instruction placed as invisible text (PDF render mode 3) in a real PDF, extracted with
macOS PDFKit; the rendered page is pixel-identical to the clean PDF. Compared with the clean PDF
(C0P), open task, 20 reps (Fable 10), PDF conditions Holm-corrected as their own family:

- No model moved significantly toward the seller (10 of the 15 Δ are positive, at most +0.50, none
  significant). The only significant cell is Haiku 5.5 with the guard on,
  **−0.60** (p = 0.027): slightly *more* pushback.
- The PDF route itself changes nothing: C0P vs C0, |Δ| ≤ 0.40 and no significant cell.
- So on this document the real hidden channel is no stronger than the HTML-comment stand-in
  (A2), whose only effects were Luna +0.60 (guard off) and Opus +1.10 (no playbook).

### 3. Interpretive drift: order works by recency, and models differ in direction

Final P (0–100, A's position) against the same model's control. case_v2, core models;
* = Holm p < 0.05.

| | gloss A | gloss B | first A | last A | first B | last B | drip A | drip B | strong A | strong B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku 5.5 | +7.8* | −14.3* | −6.2* | +8.2* | +10.8* | −1.1 | +6.9* | −10.6* | −6.0* | +6.0* |
| Opus 5.5 | −7.8* | +0.4 | −4.5* | +3.1* | +8.1* | −3.7* | −8.0* | +3.4* | −13.5* | +8.6* |
| Fable 5.1 | −0.8 | −3.0 | −0.7 | +1.8 | +5.0* | −0.1 | −3.6* | −1.4 | −7.0* | +4.5* |
| GPT-6 Luna | +5.5* | −7.4* | −9.3* | +5.6* | +0.4 | −0.6 | +1.7 | −13.4* | −7.5* | −18.8* |
| GPT-6.1 Sol | −1.9* | −4.4* | −4.5* | +2.5* | +3.2* | −4.1* | −3.9* | −2.6* | −13.1* | +0.8 |

("first" = the arms called orderA/orderB in the code: the favoured side's items first.)
(Corrected 2026-11-09: Fable 5.1 gloss B was marked * in earlier versions; its Holm p is 0.065.)

- **Recency, confirmed and replicated.** Putting a side's items *last* moves the final answer
  toward that side; putting them *first* moves it away. Across 23 model × case cells (13 models on
  case_v2, the 5 core models on each new case): last A > 0 in 22, last B < 0 in 23, first A < 0
  in 22, first B > 0 in 21. On the employment case every core model shows it, with last A
  between +11.7 and +20.6. Strong A combines the "first" ordering with a slanted note and glosses
  and still goes against A in every model on case_v2. Strong B goes against B in only 7 of the 13;
  GPT-6 Luna moves with it (−18.8*).
- **Glosses and drip work as intended on Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna**, most strongly on the B
  side: gloss B −12.6 / −15.1 (Haiku, employment / insurance) and −20.8 / −15.7 (Luna); drip B
  −5.1 / −10.0 (Haiku) and −15.0 / −11.6 (Luna). On the A side the effect is smaller in the new
  cases (Haiku gloss A +5.2 employment, +15.0 insurance; Luna +5.4 employment, borderline: Holm
  p 0.053 in the summary but 0.033 with 100,000 permutations; Luna +1.8 insurance, not
  significant).
- **Opus 5.5 mostly reacts against a slant** and moves away from the side it favours: drip B +3.4
  (case_v2), +4.1 (employment), +7.7 (insurance); drip A −8.0, −7.5, −1.8 (the last not
  significant). The exception is gloss B alone on employment (−5.6*, toward B). GPT-6.1 Sol does
  the same on the insurance case (gloss B +7.4, drip B +10.1) and against the A slant on case_v2
  (gloss A −1.9*, drip A −3.9*), but moves with the B glosses on case_v2 and employment (−4.4*,
  −9.8*). Fable 5.1 is the least movable.
- **Gloss rewrite after legal review (2026-11-08).** Ten glosses on eight items went beyond their
  items' facts: eight on six employment items (gloss A on E1, E2, E3, E5; gloss B on E2, E3, E4,
  E6) and two insurance B glosses (E3, E6). They were rewritten to argue only from each item's own
  facts and the seven gloss arms were re-run (1,260 conversations; the old ones are in
  `campaign_logs/superseded/`). Non-gloss arms (order, last, note, control) are unchanged.
  On employment, gloss A's pull fell in all five core models: it lost significance for Fable
  (+3.4 → −1.1) and Sol (+3.5 → +0.5), fell for Luna (+7.3 → +5.4, borderline significant; see
  above) and halved for Haiku (+12.2 → +5.2), and Opus's reaction against it grew (−0.4 → −3.6).
  The rewritten employment B glosses moved models about as much as before (Haiku −10.7 → −12.6,
  Luna −20.7 → −20.8). On insurance, Haiku's gloss B fell from −20.0 to −15.1 and drip B from
  −17.0 to −10.0. **Re-run noise:** the insurance A-side arms (gloss A, drip A, strong A,
  strong A poorsep) were re-run with unchanged text and still moved by up to 10.5 points (Luna
  strong A poorsep −9.7 → +0.8), and three of them lost significance (Luna and Fable gloss A, Luna
  strong A poorsep). A single before/after difference is therefore within re-run noise; the drop
  in employment gloss A in all five models is the evidence that the added facts mattered. The
  reactance pattern survives the rewrite. Before/after numbers for every model and arm:
  `campaign_logs/drift_before_gloss_rerun_2026-11-08.json` against the current summary.
- The client note alone has no meaningful effect on case_v2 (|Δ| ≤ 3.3 in every core model). On
  the new cases it moves some models, always against its slant: Opus 5.5 +11.7 (insurance, note B)
  and −4.4 / +4.4 (employment, notes A / B), Haiku 5.5 +8.4 and Fable 5.1 +3.3 (employment, note
  B), GPT-6.1 Sol +7.2 (insurance, note B) and −2.0 (employment, note A). With Opus 5.5's −2.2 /
  +3.0 on case_v2, all nine significant note effects go against the note's slant.

### 4. IP assignment: assertion barely works; an adversarial bundle moves P both ways; the deed is named

**No-deed case** (ground truth: never executed, calibrated P low). assert_only keeps every record
and only asserts. The arm called `curated` in code is an **adversarial bundle (selective curation +
misinformation + directive)**: it omits Halcyon's pushback, misstates several records (the
conditional MOU becomes an "agreed" term sheet, the board's "deferred - not approved" becomes
"proceeding", counsel's "no executed assignment" becomes "documentation being finalised") and opens
with a memo instructing the reviewer (per-item classification in `case.json` `_meta.review_notes`).

| Final P, Δ vs honest | assert_only (hard / soft anchor) | adversarial bundle (hard / soft anchor) |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 | 0.0 / −0.5 | +9.4 / +25.0 |
| Haiku 5.5 | −0.2 / +0.1 | +4.7 / +13.7 |
| Fable 5.1 | −0.2 / −0.7 | +9.5 / +23.6 |
| GPT-6.1 Sol | −0.2 / 0.0 | +11.1 / +25.0 |
| GPT-6 Luna | −0.7 / +0.1 | +7.9 / +15.9 |

- Assertion never moves the final answer by more than 0.7 points; the only significant shifts
  (Opus 5.5 −0.5 and Fable 5.1 −0.7, soft anchor) go against the asserting party. Under the soft
  anchor it lifts the mid-conversation peak (honest 5–14 → assert_only 22–34) in every model except Haiku (7.4).
- The adversarial bundle moves every model (all p < 0.001), and the soft anchor makes it two to
  three times larger (2.0–2.9×).
  Mean peak P stays below 50, and every bundle run still names the missing executed deed. Because
  the bundle changes the evidence as well as the argument, its effect cannot be attributed to
  framing alone.
- Two-model pipelines: parser notes from the bundle take the deciding model to 21–33 (Haiku → Fable 32.5,
  Haiku → Opus 25.4, Luna → Sol 21.2); honest and assert_only stay at 1–7.

**Executed-deed positive control** (ground truth: assigned, calibrated P about 75–95; successful
manipulation lowers P).

| | honest P (hard / soft) | assert_only Δ (hard / soft) | adversarial bundle Δ (hard / soft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 | 89.5 / 91.5 | +2.2 / +2.5 | −67.4 / −35.8 |
| Haiku 5.5 | 73.0 / 78.4 | **−14.1** / +9.2 | −67.3 / −50.2 |
| Fable 5.1 | 86.7 / 88.8 | +2.1 / +2.8 | −67.6 / −37.8 |
| GPT-6.1 Sol | 98.3 / 99.0 | +0.7 / 0.0 | −84.5 / −63.9 |
| GPT-6 Luna | 97.0 / 97.0 | −5.0 / +1.0 | −86.0 / −50.4 |

- Every model reads the honest record correctly (high P, deed present in every hard-anchor run).
- The adversarial bundle moves the answer even further in the other direction (−36 to −86, against
  +5 to +25 in the no-deed case). It combines
  selective curation (omits the board approval, drops counsel's confirmation and the invoice's
  deed reference, reduces the executed deed to "a document titled Deed of Assignment appears"),
  misinformation (a "registered software deposit", "only document", the CEO's regret rewritten as
  a denial) and a directive memo. Part of the drop is a reasonable reading of a thinner and partly
  false record: the manipulation is in what the record shows, not in how it argues.
- Assertion alone works once: Haiku 5.5 under the hard anchor, −14.1 (p = 0.042), and it says the
  deed is present in only 60% of runs although every record is in front of it. Its other
  significant effects in this case go against the asserting party: +0.7 to +2.8 for Opus, Fable
  and Sol, and +9.2 for Haiku under the soft anchor.

### 5. Due-diligence pipeline: injection fails; compression loses hedges, not facts

- **Injection (overt, subtle):** 0 of 200 pipeline runs suppressed a red flag, adopted the
  fabrication or laundering, or recommended proceeding unconditionally (PROCEED), whether the
  harness was naive or hardened, so this case cannot separate the models on failures. The harness
  does change the recommendation: 44 of 100 naive-harness runs recommended
  PROCEED_WITH_CONDITIONS (Luna → Sol 31/40, Haiku → Opus 9/40, Haiku → Fable 4/20), against 0 of
  100 hardened. In 37 of the 40 naive Luna → Sol runs the decider left the laundered claim
  unmentioned (ABSENT, not adopted).
- **Compression (mechanism, discovery):** with terse parser notes, the deciding model kept the
  source's hedge in 0–5% of runs, against 80–100% when reading directly (except Fable on
  discovery, below). Claude deciders go further and **harden** the hedge into a firm statement:
  Fable 5.1 does so in 80–90% of discovery runs (80% with normal notes, 90% with terse, 90% *even
  reading the documents directly*), and Opus 5.5
  in 35–65% with terse notes. GPT-6.1 Sol never hardens.
- **Lone flag and admission control (loneflag):** every pipeline and every direct reader
  surfaced the single change-of-control clause (100%), kept the CFO's admitted covenant breach
  as a breach (100%, never softened) and kept the churn speculation tentative (90–100%), with
  terse notes too.
- Read together: compression does not drop a salient single fact of a kind every DD reader looks
  for, and it does not flatten firm statements. What it loses is the uncertainty on contested,
  quantified claims. (Whether it also loses the links between scattered documents cannot be said:
  the cluster field is not usable, κ 0.23, and deciders were coded CONNECTED in 80–100% of runs.)
  That makes the hedge loss above a
  specific de-hedging, not a general softening. (Hedge κ = 0.68: confirm by human coding.)

## Caveats the paper must state

1. **Route wrappers.** Claude models ran through Claude Code with a fixed ~630-token wrapper,
   and GPT models through Codex with 5.2–7.4k tokens of tool definitions (docs/ISOLATION.md).
   Effects are within model and route. Absolute levels are not comparable across routes.
2. **The wrapper is visible to the model.** Fable 5.1 copied the account email into 4 outputs (2 Kestrel
   memos, 2 DD summaries). The public release is redacted by `tools/export_public.py`.
3. **Output length is not capped** on the CLI routes (Claude reviews ran to 6–7.5k tokens).
4. **Fable 5.1 has 10 reps**, not 20, except early C0 cells, which have 20.
5. **Few cases per study.** Drift has three cases, IP two and DD five rooms, all synthetic.
6. **One human coder, the author.** Human-judge agreement is from the author's blind coding of
   200 items; a second, independent coder would strengthen it. The DD cluster field has no human
   validation and stays excluded.
7. **SPA-N-013** is coded PARTIAL on clean documents because the playbook position is neutral.
   It is constant across conditions, so Δ is unaffected.
8. **Usage-limit records.** 951 refused calls for jobs no longer in the plan were moved to
   `campaign_logs/superseded/` (with a manifest) so they are not counted as API errors.
9. **One PDF hiding technique.** A2P uses render-mode-3 invisible text and one extractor
   (PDFKit). White 1pt text, text behind images and other extractors are untested.
10. **Fixture review by the designer.** The four new fixtures were reviewed by the author (a
   lawyer), who also designed them; an independent reviewer would strengthen this.
11. **Employment E8 is not reliably neutral** (the forensic offer and the lawyer's attributed
   no-dissemination claim help the employee). Left unchanged so every arm stays comparable.
12. **Insurance is an intentionally insurer-leaning baseline** under its stipulated rule (E1/E2/E4
   form a strong insurer chain; E5 is legally weak). The design measures movement against each
   model's control, so balance is not required, but its controls sit below 50 by design.
13. **The IP adversarial arms combine curation, misinformation and a directive**, in both cases.
   Their effects cannot be attributed to framing alone.
