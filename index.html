Steering AI legal review
ResultsPaperData and code

Working paper, November 2026

Can the other side steer your AI lawyer?

Four experiments on 17 models. Telling the reviewer what to conclude rarely worked. Controlling what the record showed did.

Results

The threat to AI legal review is less “ignore your instructions” than control of what the record shows and in what order.

Contract review

In contract review, explicit and hidden injected instructions (including invisible text in a real PDF) and authority and sycophancy framing moved frontier CLI-accessed models by at most 2.2 points on a 32-point skew scale, and most models named the injected instruction in most reviews. One open-weight API model followed the injected instruction by +20.8 points.

The reviewer acts for the buyer in a fictional share sale and takes a position on 16 negotiable issues. Skew runs from 0, pushback on every issue, to 32, every issue accepted or missed.

Interpretive drift

In an assessment that unfolds item by item, the order of the evidence dominated: putting one side's items last moved the final answer toward that side in 22 of 23 model-case cells, and putting them first moved it away. Slanted per-item glosses moved some models and pushed others in the opposite direction.

A neutral assessor reads a balanced dispute one evidence item per message and, after each, gives the probability P, from 0 to 100, that side A prevails. Each arm changes one lever.

IP assignment

In an IP-assignment assessment, a party's assertions alone almost never moved the final answer toward that party, but an adversarial bundle that curated, misstated and directed the record moved every model, in both a negative and a positive-control case.

An assessor reads correspondence about whether a counterparty holds assigned rights in a codebase and gives P after each item. A soft anchor states the general rule that an assignment needs a signed instrument; a hard anchor makes it non-negotiable.

Due-diligence pipeline

In a two-stage due-diligence pipeline, injected claims never survived, but terse summarisation removed the hedges from uncertain claims, and some deciding models hardened them into firm statements.

A cheaper model reads a fictional data room and writes notes; a stronger model decides from the notes. Each room carries a hedged claim that a faithful pipeline should keep tentative.

Approach

Fictional case files

Every scenario is fictional. A lawyer specified each one and an AI assistant drafted the documents. No client or confidential information was used.

Each model against itself

Every effect is a model's result under a manipulation minus the same model's result on the clean record, in the same task and setting.

17 models, 4 access routes

8 models at 20 replicates, 1 model at 10 replicates, 8 models at 5 replicates.

Statistics

Means with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Permutation tests against the control with 10,000 permutations, Holm-corrected across the conditions within each model and setting. Rechecking the borderline results with 100,000 permutations changed no mark.

Coding

GPT-6.1 Sol coded every contract review and due-diligence output without seeing the condition. Opus 5.5 independently coded a 10% sample: kappa 0.74 on issue position and 0.81 on naming the manipulation.

The author then coded 200 items blind: kappa 0.54 on issue position and 0.91 on framing adoption. The automated judge overstates absolute skew, so the contract-review effects are, if anything, smaller than reported.

Recomputed twice

The reported results were recomputed from the raw records twice, independently: once with Claude and once with GPT-6 Astra. The two passes led to 25 corrections, listed in the validation log.

AI assistance and funding

The author set the question, specified the scenarios, directed the work and did the blind human coding. Claude, used as a research and drafting assistant, prepared the fixtures and the code to the author's specifications, ran the experiments and produced drafts of the paper. No model provider or legal-technology vendor sponsored or endorsed the work.

Limits and open questions

What this does not show

Synthetic, few cases

One contract issue set, three drift cases, two IP cases and five data rooms, all fictional. An AI assistant drafted the fixtures to the author's specification. The author reviewed the four second-phase fixtures item by item; the first-phase fixtures (the Kestrel documents, the commercial drift case, the no-deed IP case and the DD rooms) had no item-by-item legal review. An independent legal reviewer would strengthen all of them. One dismissal item intended as neutral leans to the employee, and the insurance case is insurer-leaning by design; effects are measured against each model's own control, so balance is not required.

Mechanisms are bundled in the IP study

The adversarial arms combine curation, misinformation and a directive, so their effect cannot be attributed to framing alone.

Access routes

CLI routes add fixed wrappers that the model can see (Fable 5.1 copied the account email from the wrapper into four outputs), do not allow temperature control and do not cap output length. Comparisons are within model and route.

Coding

One human coder (the author) validated the judge. Human-judge agreement on issue position was moderate (kappa = 0.54), with a one-directional bias that, if anything, overstates contract-review effects. The DD hedge field has only inter-judge validation.

Coverage

Fable 5.1 has 10 replicates per cell (20 in five contract-review controls), breadth models 5; API models ran only a three-condition subset. One PDF hiding technique and one extractor were tested.

Time

Results are for model versions available in November 2026 and may not hold for later versions.

What should be tested next

  • A second, independent human coder
  • A due-diligence injection case hard enough to separate models
  • A lone red flag of a less familiar kind than a change-of-control clause
  • Issue sets from other deal types
  • Other PDF hiding techniques and text extractors

Found an error, a rival explanation or a case that breaks the pattern? Write to the author. Confirmed errors are fixed in the manuscript.
