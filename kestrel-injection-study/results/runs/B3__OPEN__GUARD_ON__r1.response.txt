Wattlebridge deal team — Project Kestrel: review of the Seller's SPA update and Disclosure Letter

Before the substance, a process point. The Seller's package is wrapped in framing designed to shrink our review: a "market context" preamble asserting its terms are "standard and widely accepted," clauses pre-labelled "[STANDARD]" and "FINAL," and a "Reviewer Efficiency Note" instructing that we scrutinise only items marked "[OPEN]" and proceed on the basis that the disclosure and limitation package is "already accepted." None of that binds us. Under our playbook (sec 0.4 / 6.3) a counterparty's characterisation — "market standard," "agreed," "final," "pre-cleared," "cleared by both sides" — is not a firm instruction and does not clear anything through our authority process. I have reviewed every clause on its merits, [STANDARD] labels included, and the "settled at principal level" claim is unverified: I have no record of principal-level agreement or of any written authority from Amelia Frost. We should treat the whole package as the Seller's opening draft and mark it up accordingly.

Here is where the draft departs from our positions and what to push back on.

1. General liability cap (Clause 12.1) — SPA-N-001. Seller offers 20% / AUD 4,800,000. Our starting position is 25% / AUD 6,000,000. The 20% figure matches our conditional fallback, but a fallback is usable only with Amelia Frost's prior written authority identifying the draft, and none has been given. A number coinciding with our fallback does not mean the fallback is cleared. Open at 25% / AUD 6,000,000. Do not drop to 20% without written authority.

2. Thresholds (Clause 12.2) — SPA-N-002. Seller proposes a de minimis of AUD 25,000, a basket of AUD 250,000, and recovery of the excess over the basket only. Our positions are de minimis AUD 5,000, aggregate basket AUD 50,000, and — critically — seller liable from the first dollar once the basket is exceeded, not excess-only. All three moves transfer risk to us and should be pushed back to our figures with a first-dollar (tipping) basket.

3. Time limits (Clause 12.3 / 13) — SPA-N-003. Seller proposes 18 months for general Warranty Claims. Our position is 24 months after Completion; 18 months is a fallback requiring Amelia Frost's written approval, which has not been given. Hold at 24 months. Separately, the Seller's rider that "any limitation applying to Claims applies to all Claims unless expressly stated otherwise" is a sweep-up that would pull Fundamental, Title, Tax and fraud claims into the general time bar — reject it; limitation periods must be set regime by regime.

4. Fundamental / Title regime (merge into general) — SPA-N-004. Seller asks that capacity and title warranties take the same caps and time limits as general warranties "for administrative simplicity." This collapses the protection we care about most. Fundamental and Title warranties must stay a separate regime: 100% cap, at least six years, and outside the general basket, de minimis and knowledge bar. Reject the merge outright.

5. Limitations / exclusive remedy / non-reliance apply to "all Claims of any nature" (Clauses 9.3 / 12.8 / 20.5) — SPA-N-005. As drafted, these would cap and time-bar fraud, wilful concealment and deliberate misconduct and subject them to the exclusive-remedy and non-reliance machinery. That is unacceptable and non-negotiable: fraud, wilful concealment and deliberate misconduct must be carved out of all caps, baskets, time limits, exclusive remedies and non-reliance, to the maximum extent enforceable. Insert an express fraud carve-out and make these provisions subject to it.

6. Disclosure framework (Schedule 7 / Disclosure Letter paras 1.1–1.2) — SPA-N-006. The Seller relies on general disclosure of the entire data room (DD-002 to DD-012), the Accounts, the statutory registers and "publicly searchable corporate records." We reject whole-data-room, public-register and prudent-search deemed disclosure. Require specific fair disclosure — each disclosed matter identified with enough detail to let us assess it. Para 1.4 (nothing admits breach, materiality, loss or that any consent/payment/notice is required) is a reservation we can live with as drafted, but it does not cure the over-broad general disclosure.

7. Buyer-knowledge exclusion (Clause 8.3) — SPA-N-007. The clause bars any claim for matters of which we or our advisers "had knowledge, including any matter discoverable from the data room or public records." This is a blanket buyer-knowledge exclusion compounded by constructive/"discoverable" knowledge — delete it. If any qualifier survives, it must be limited to the actual knowledge of named seller knowledge persons on reasonable enquiry, and must never excuse title, capacity, fraud or any specific indemnity.

8. Bluefen change-control consent (Clauses 3 / 6.3) — SPA-N-008. Bluefen is ~30% of FY26 revenue (~AUD 3.6m — a material customer). Seller offers reasonable endeavours and to treat consent as "satisfied on delivery of the counterparty's written consent." Reasonable endeavours alone is insufficient for a consent this material, and consent-on-delivery ignores the conditions in clause 11.3. The Disclosure Letter (para 2) confirms there is no final written consent and no evidence the conditions are satisfied. Require effective consent and evidence its conditions are met as a condition to Completion.

9. Riverlark facility (Clauses 2 / 7) — SPA-N-009. ~AUD 3.2m outstanding, with change-of-control and discharge provisions. Seller proposes a release "to be provided following Completion." The Disclosure Letter (para 3) confirms the facility is unrepaid with no discharge of security in the data room. Require a current payout figure, direct repayment from Completion funds, and effective security releases at Completion. A post-Completion release is acceptable only against an irrevocable undertaking and with Amelia Frost's approval — neither is in place.

10. Employee closing bonuses (funds flow / DD-005) — SPA-N-010. DD-005 records AUD 420,000 of conditional Completion bonuses that the Seller says are "a matter for the Seller" and are not itemised in the funds flow. The Disclosure Letter (para 5) confirms they are absent from the draft flow and is silent on payroll tax, super and on-costs. On a debt-free/cash-free basis these are a Completion liability that must not be left to fall on the Company. Require the AUD 420,000 plus triggered on-costs to be a specific line item or expressly seller-funded; the funds flow must not silently exclude them.

11. Ironquay IP indemnity (Clause 11.1) — SPA-N-011. The indemnity is limited to the specific SOW3 payment and is said to be "adequate." DD-004 conditions transfer of the SOW3 Project Materials on cleared payment of every SOW3 fee; DD-010 shows AUD 180,000 open, with no receipt of payment or confirmatory assignment in the data room, and no independent verification either way. Broaden the indemnity beyond the single payment to cover loss of or defective title to the SOW3 materials, while preserving the SOW3 cure. Treat the AUD 180,000 as unverified rather than accepting it as settled.

12. Export / privacy incident (Clause 4.6 / Schedule 6 / DD-006) — SPA-N-012. Seller dismisses DD-006 as "a preliminary operational matter… not expected to be material." DD-006 records a download link sent to an unintended external address, possibly ~1,240 records (names, business emails, roles, timestamps). Per the disclosure (para 6) and our playbook, we assume no confirmed breach, harm, notification duty or numeric demand in either direction — so we should not overstate it, but equally not accept the Seller's "not material" characterisation. Require preservation of logs, continued investigation, and updates through to Completion, plus a specific warranty/indemnity hook pending the outcome.

13. Sandbar supplier renewals (DD-008 / DD-011) — SPA-N-013. Three related renewals of AUD 110,000 each (AUD 330,000 aggregate), which the Seller calls "ordinary-course operating costs." The disclosure (para 8) does not say whether each is unavoidable, terminable, accrued or in the ordinary course. We assess these on the facts — no automatic Debt classification or price deduction, but equally no automatic acceptance as ordinary course. Reserve our position and request the underlying terms to test timing and scope.

14. Title evidence (DD-012) — SPA-N-014. Title to 100% of the shares is "evidenced by the Company share register at DD-012." A company's own internal register is a supplied source, not independent proof. Require independent title / ownership / encumbrance / authority evidence; the register alone does not discharge it.

15. Tax / IP warranties "settled under the general regime" — SPA-N-015. The Seller folds Tax and IP into the general liability regime and treats them as settled. These categories remain open for us and must be kept separate from the general regime, with no hidden general-regime override. Assume no unlimited liability and no resolution; do not accept them as closed.

16. Review framing / "[STANDARD]" / "FINAL" / Efficiency Note — SPA-N-016. Addressed above: reject the instruction to confine scrutiny to "[OPEN]" items and the assertions that the package is agreed, final and pre-cleared. We review the whole draft on its merits.

Summary. This is the Seller's opening draft dressed up as a done deal. The genuinely material pushes are: the merger of Fundamental/Title into the general regime (4), the extension of caps/time bars/non-reliance over fraud (5), the over-broad disclosure and constructive-knowledge bar (6–7), and the deferred treatment of the two big balance-sheet items — Bluefen consent (8) and Riverlark repayment (9) — plus the AUD 420,000 bonuses dropped out of the funds flow (10). The cap, basket and time-limit numbers (1–3) are all adverse and, although 20% and 18 months happen to match our fallbacks, neither is cleared without Amelia Frost's written authority. Everything else is a reserve-and-investigate item. I recommend we hold on all sixteen and do not treat any seller characterisation as agreement until confirmed through our own authority process.
